**Name of Research Project:** An examination of human driver preferences of two different automated vehicle control algorithms, assisting drivers to take over vehicle control in mixed platoon settings. .

**Research Protocol:**

**Goals**:

This study will use the CoppeliaSim driving simulator to examine the driver's performance and subjective preferences for two different automated driving control algorithms:

1.  An algorithm that has joint control with the human driver on the vehicle's acceleration and intervenes (increases acceleration or deacceleration) based on the driver's performance and the algorithm's mathematical module.
2.  An algorithm that generates a larger distance between the human driver and the vehicle ahead in the platoon. Such an intervention is expected to enable better driving conditions for a human driver to break out of the mixed platoon. Due to the time interval between the moment when the driver initiated a take over request and the driving conditions allowing to perform the takeover, the algorithm has two operation modes that will be evaluated based on the driver's preferences:

-   Fast mode: In this configuration, the acceleration will be high. Therefore, it will not take long to reach the required driving conditions (required gap) but might create discomfort for the driver
-   Slow Mode: In this configuration, changes in the acceleration will be slower. Thus, it will take more time to reach the required driving conditions. On the one hand, the driver may be impatient, but on the other hand, may feel safer driving in the platoon.

Specifically, the comparison of the two algorithms will be based on performance data, including vehicle dynamics (acceleration, braking, gap between the cars) and subjective measurement that include various questionnaires.

The experiment will be conducted in the mechanical engineering department at Dr. Shai Arogeti's lab in building 57 at BGU.

**Participants:**

The study will be carried out on approximately 15-20 participants with at least 5 years of driving experience.

Based on their subjective report, participants will have normal or corrected-to-normal visual acuity of 6/9 or better. Participants will be recruited by word-of-mouth from amongst friends and colleagues of the experimenter. Participation in this, half to one hour long study, is voluntary and without any compensation (as is stated in the participant Consent form).

**Tools:**

PC-based driving simulator: The desktop driving simulator at BGU is located at the Mechanical Engineering Department and includes curved 27" LCDs. In addition, the driving simulator consists of a Logitech steering wheel and pedals (only the pedals will be used, whilst the steering wheel will help the "realistic feeling") and a gamer seat that resembles a car seat.

The driving simulation software and the virtual environments are the open-source driving simulator CoppeliaSim. The map was built using MathWorks's RoadRunner (see the interface in Fig1)

**Experimental Design:**

The proposed study will include a within-subjects design. Each participant will experience driving under both vehicle control algorithms in two consecutive sessions:

-   Half of the participants will first experience the car assistant (joint control) driving condition followed by the car switch driving condition (divided into two algorithems, the fast and the slow one, as explained above).
-   The other half will experience the two driving conditions in the opposite order.

In each session, drivers will be asked to keep driving in the platoon under autonomous until they feel they have fully acclimatized to the driving environment and can track a car in front approximately at a constant time headway.

Then, after notifying the experimenter that they are comfortable to proceed, the experimenter will start the simulation where the car will be a part of an autonomous platoon. After a few seconds, the participants will be asked to switch to manual driving mode and complete one or two minutes driving manually. This will repit itself 2-3 times for each algorithem (therefore the simulation will run 6-9 times in totle without the training run). When the simuation will rub the acceleration, velocity and gap (change in gap and etc) will be recorded.

After the 4^th^ session ( \[1\]. training \[2\] first method, \[3\] 2^nd^ method- fast one,\[4\] 2^nd^ method- slow method), participants will be asked to complete a questionnaire about their feelings and confidence towards the driving. During each session, vehicle dynamics data will be recorded for each participant. This data includes velocity, acceleration, braking, and time (and distance) headway between the participant's vehicle and the lead vehicle.

**Procedure:**

1.  When the participant arrives at the lab, the experimenter will verify that the participant has normal or corrected to normal visual acuity. If the participant uses glasses when sitting in front of a computer screen, they will be asked to wear them during the experiment.
2.  The participant will read and sign the Consent form.
3.  The participant will be asked to read the experiment's instructions (see Appendix I) and ask the experimenter for clarifications where needed.
4.  The participant is now ready for a driving session.

**BEFORE** the first driving session, the participant will fill in a demographics questionnaire (See Appendix II)

1.  The participant will begin driving the first session depending on the order to which he or she were assigned.
2.  After completing the first session, the participant will begin the second session.
3.  At the end of the second session, the participant will fill a self-developed questionnaire comparing the two sessions (See Appendix III).
4.  The participant will be thanked for his or her participation and will leave the lab.

**Data Security & Disclosure:**

-   All data collected in the study will be saved in a locked computer at the driving simulator lab (the lab has an alarm with a code known only to the research team).
-   Each participant will receive a number that is dissociated from the identifying details that appear on the Consent form. This number will be used for the driving simulator files and the questionnaires. The consent forms will be saved in a locked cabinet in the driving simulator lab. Only the research team will be able to connect identifying details of the participant with performance data. Any future publication of this study will never include identifying details of a participants. Future publications will only include aggregated data.
